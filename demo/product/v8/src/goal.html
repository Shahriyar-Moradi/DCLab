Goal & test design

What we want to predict, what “good” means, and how we test it fairly. They are set when a project starts a run; this screen shows them.

What we predict

churned_next_30d

Will this customer cancel in the next 30 days? A yes/no answer (binary classification). One answer per customer-month. Prediction horizon: 30 days.

How we rank models

PR-AUC

Only about 1 in 5 training rows has the answer the model treats as yes, and PR-AUC rewards finding them without flagging many others.

Business rule

Recall ≥ 0.80

Catch at least 80% of the customers who will cancel. The model in use met it on cross-validation.

Time-ordered by snapshot_month. Fold 1 learns from the first 6,551 rows and is checked on the next 6,551; each later fold learns from more of the past, up to 32,755 rows in fold 5. The final test set is the latest 8,904 rows (18%). Shuffled rows would let the model peek at the future.

No dates are shown here, only rows: where the final test set starts in time is part of what it keeps sealed.

What a time-ordered design rules out. The “beats the noise” comparison and the improvement loop assume folds that are not ordered in time, so on this project two runs can be compared side by side but not judged against the noise. See Improve.
